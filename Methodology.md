# HELIOS Methodology

HELIOS uses a structured risk-intelligence framework to organize signals across multiple dimensions rather than treating any single indicator as a definitive predictor.

## Risk dimensions

### Economic
- GDP growth and economic momentum
- Inflation and price stability
- Employment conditions
- Trade and fiscal indicators

### Political and institutional
- Political stability
- Governance indicators
- Institutional resilience
- Major political developments

### Environmental
- Climate exposure
- Natural-disaster activity
- Resource and environmental stress

### Social
- Demographic pressures
- Migration trends
- Social stability signals

### Strategic events
- Conflict and security developments
- Supply-chain disruptions
- Major regional developments

## Data sources

HELIOS is designed to use publicly available structured and unstructured information. Relevant source families include international economic and development datasets, public event reporting, and other reputable open-data sources. Examples include the IMF, World Bank, United Nations, and public geopolitical reporting.

## Risk scoring

HELIOS combines normalized signals into composite assessments intended to support comparison and monitoring. Scores are presented on a 0–100 scale and should be interpreted as analytical indicators rather than guarantees of future outcomes.

## AI-assisted analysis

AI can assist with signal classification, summarization, trend detection, and report generation. AI outputs are intended to support analysis and should be evaluated alongside source data and human judgment.

## Transparency and limitations

Global risk is dynamic and inherently uncertain. HELIOS assessments can be affected by data availability, reporting delays, model assumptions, and sudden developments. The platform is a decision-support tool, not a substitute for professional judgment.
